# Primary action: from how customers actually buy

**Load at:** Phase 1 (business sites, with `strategic-loops.md`), Phase 3 (the paragraph's hero and action), Phase 4 (first frame), Phase 7 (the review's primary-action check).

**The rule:** the primary action is the thing this business's customers do to become customers. It is decided from how they buy, never from habit. **The phone number is not the default primary action.** "Call us" at the top of every small-business site is a habit, and it is wrong wherever customers order, browse, book or look something up before they ever speak to anyone. A florist whose customers pick a bouquet and order for tomorrow does not need a giant phone button. A plumber with water on the floor does.

Phone numbers stay correct, visible where a person would look for one (header, footer, contact, the hours block, the confirmation) and always a tappable `tel:` link on phones. They just do not lead the first frame unless the table below says calling is how this business is bought.

## How to decide

Ask in discovery, in the owner's words, never as "what is your CTA":

1. **When someone becomes a customer, what exactly happens?** (They walk in. They order online. They book a slot. They send photos and get a number. They phone because something is broken.)
2. **What does the customer want to see before they commit?** (The menu. The flowers. Prices. Availability. Finished jobs. Reviews.)
3. **What is the deadline or constraint?** (Same-day delivery cutoff. Slots fill by Thursday. Pre-order by 8 pm. Emergency.)
4. **How often do they need the phone?** (Almost never; only for custom work; always.)

Then write the primary action as a verb plus its object ("Order tomorrow's bouquets by noon"), and decide **what the first frame must let a visitor do without a click** (see the hero catalogue in `hero.md`).

## Business type, primary action, and where the phone belongs

The concept decides how each of these looks. This table decides what must be primary.

| Business | Primary action | What the first frame carries | Where the phone belongs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Florist** | Browse and order bouquets (online, or build one) | The flowers; a way to pick one; same-day delivery cutoff stated plainly ("Order by noon for today") | Footer, contact, delivery questions, weddings and sympathy (custom work). A plain tappable link, not the hero button |
| **Bakery** | See today's bake and pre-order | What is out of the oven today; pre-order cutoff; hours | Hours block and footer; large-order enquiries |
| **Restaurant, café, bar** | Menu first, then hours, then order or reserve | The food; open or closed now; the order or reservation link (their real system, or "call to reserve" if that is the only way) | If reservations are by phone only, it is primary and says so. Otherwise footer and contact |
| **Food truck, takeout** | Where and when, then order | Today's location and hours; menu | Order line if that is how they take orders |
| **Plumber, electrician, HVAC, roofer (scheduled work)** | Request an estimate, with photos | A short form that takes a photo and a ZIP; response time; licence and insurance | Visible in the header and footer; a text option. Not a giant button |
| **Emergency trade (burst pipe, lockout, tow, 24-hour repair)** | **Call.** This one is genuinely primary | A call control that is the largest thing on screen on a phone; service area; "24/7" only if true | Primary, everywhere, one tap. Still not the only content |
| **Landscaper, cleaner, mover, painter** | Request a quote (form or text) | Finished work; areas served; quote form | Header and contact |
| **Salon, barber, groomer, spa** | Book a slot | The booking strip or the real booking link; services with durations and prices | Footer and contact; change requests |
| **Gym, studio, martial arts, class** | Book a first class or trial | The schedule; a first-class slot picker | Contact; the front desk hours |
| **Clinic, dentist, therapist** | Book an appointment or request a call back | Hours; new-patient information; insurance line | Header and contact (some patients will only call), alongside online booking |
| **Retail, boutique, gift shop** | Browse, then visit | A few real products; hours; address; parking | Footer; stock questions |
| **Venue, event space, caterer** | Check a date, request a quote | Photographs of the space; a date picker or enquiry form | Contact; the planner's direct line |
| **Professional services (accountant, lawyer, agent)** | Book a consultation or ask a question | What happens at the first meeting; credentials | Contact; header |
| **Auto repair, shop** | Request service or a drop-off time | The work shown; hours; drop-off slots | Header and contact. Call primary only if they truly take bookings by phone |
| **Nonprofit, church, club** | The one thing they ask of visitors (give, join, come Sunday) | Times and place; one clear ask | Contact |

When a business sits between two rows, take the one that matches how *its* customers behave, and ask the owner.

## What to put in the first frame instead of a phone button

- **Something to do:** a picker, a booking strip, a builder, a slot grid, a menu you can filter, an order button tied to a specific item ("Add the rye, $9").
- **A deadline or availability that is true:** "Order by noon for today", "Thursday has two chairs left" (real data or clearly marked sample data, never a fake counter).
- **The real thing for sale:** the hero photograph is the product (`hero.md`, `photography.md`).

## Where the phone number goes (when it is not primary)

- In the header as a quiet text link with a small icon, or in the footer.
- In the contact section, set with the hours.
- In the confirmation ("We got your order. Questions? Call 555 0100").
- Always `tel:` on a phone, with the number formatted as people read it, and correct (verify it against the client's real number).
- On phones, a compact call button in a sticky bar is fine as a secondary control, and is expected for any business where customers sometimes call. It is not a replacement for the real primary action.

## Writing the action

- A verb and an object, specific ("Build a bouquet", "See today's bake", "Request an estimate", "Pick a time"). No "Get started", "Learn more", "Contact us" as a primary.
- Never two equal-weight buttons. One primary action, and a text link for the next best thing.
- The action's *form* comes from the concept (`strategic-loops.md`): a booking tab on the board, an order slip, a "Reserve" plaque, an "Add to the table" control. It must still read instantly as an action.

## Review

In the first-frame test and the report, state: the business's primary action (from the table, in one sentence), what the visitor can do in the first frame without a click, and where the phone appears. If the phone is the largest control and the business is not an emergency trade, that is a FAIL (`review.md`).
