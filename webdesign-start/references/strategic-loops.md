# Strategy essentials for business sites

**Load at:** Phase 1, for any site that has to do a job (book, sell, call, sign up). Skip it for pure showcases and art pieces.

A beautiful site that hides the hours or buries the booking button has failed its owner. This file holds the few strategic decisions a business site needs. Gather them inside the normal discovery rounds, with no extra interviews, and record them in the brief's "Business essentials" section. (The filename is kept for compatibility with older installs' updater. It replaced the earlier seven-loop process.)

## The five decisions

1. **The visitor:** who arrives, from where (search, Instagram, word of mouth, a flyer), and what they already know.
2. **The primary action:** the one thing that matters most. Every page makes it easy, and the concept styles it as its signature control: a tear-off order slip, a stamped booking card, a lit "Reserve" plaque. It must still read instantly as a button.
3. **The promise:** in one sentence, why this business rather than another. It's usually built from the particulars ("bread from a 40-year-old starter, baked since 3 a.m.").
4. **Real proof:** only what exists, such as real reviews (quoted with permission and source), years in business, awards, press, certifications, and real numbers. If there is none yet, the site leans on specificity and craft, and the report lists "proof to collect". Never invent it.
5. **The three-second test:** a stranger looking at the first frame should know what this is, feel the intended feeling, and see where to act. The concept's art supports this; it never hides it.

## Must-have content by site type

The concept decides *how* these look (a price list can be an enamel sign or a ledger plate), but they must be present and easy to find.

| Type | Must be easy to find |
|---|---|
| Restaurant, café, bakery, bar | Hours, address with a map link, menu with prices, how to order or reserve, phone, dietary notes |
| Hotel, venue, travel | Rooms or spaces with prices or "from" prices, availability or booking, location, what's included, policies |
| Local service or trade | Service area, services with a sense of price, how to get a quote, response time, licences and insurance, phone |
| Clinic, legal, financial | Credentials, what happens at a first appointment, fees or how fees work, booking, privacy reassurance, accessibility of the premises |
| Shop or product | Product, price, variants, shipping and returns, add-to-cart that is always reachable, sizing or specs |
| Software or SaaS | What it does in one line, who it's for, how it works, pricing, security or compliance notes, sign-up or demo |
| Portfolio or studio | The work first (big and fast), what the person or studio does, availability, contact |
| Event or launch | Date, time and time zone, place, price, how to get tickets, what to expect, schedule |
| Nonprofit | The cause in one line, what a gift does, how to give or join, transparency (reports, registration) |
| Publication | Latest work, subscribe, about, archive or search |

## Conversion basics the concept must respect

- The primary action is visible in the first frame on desktop and within one scroll on phones. It repeats at natural decision points.
- Navigation to the must-haves takes one step from anywhere. The nav can be an elevator dial or a ledger index, but it must be obvious and keyboard-accessible.
- Forms ask only for what's needed and show errors next to the field.
- Phone numbers and addresses are tappable on phones.
- Motion never delays reaching the action. Intros are skippable or shorter than about 1.5s.
- Page weight and speed stay in budget (`build-standards.md`). A slow site loses the visitors the art was meant to win.

## After launch (optional, when the user asks)

Offer a short follow-up list: connect analytics for the primary action, collect real reviews and photos, replace placeholders, and check the site on the owner's own phone. Keep it to a handful of concrete items with owners, not a plan document.
