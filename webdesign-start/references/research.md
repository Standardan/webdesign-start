# Research: learning from references without cloning them

**Load at:** when the user names sites or things they love, or asks for inspiration, and optionally in Phase 2 to study a technique.

References are **ingredients**. A site the user loves contributes traits (the way a photo fills the screen, the quiet, the scroll animation), and the concept absorbs those traits into its own world. Copying a reference's layout, copy, logo or imagery produces a clone. Ignoring what the user said they love produces a site they don't want. Do neither.

## When the user names things they love

1. **Record each trait in their own words**, split into single traits ("big photo" and "slow scroll" are two traits). Keep a short list in the Discovery Notes:
   `stripe.com: "the way the gradient moves behind everything"`.
2. **If you can open the site, look before describing it.** Note what actually creates the trait: scale, density, light, motion, type treatment. If you can't open it, work from the user's description and never invent observations.
3. **Translate each trait into the concept's own terms.** "The gradient moving behind everything" becomes, for a bakery ledger, "slow warm light drifting across the paper". The trait survives, and the source doesn't show.
4. **Make sure every loved trait appears somewhere** in the concept or the paragraph. In the Phase 3 digest, tell the user where each one went. If a trait conflicts with the chosen concept, say so and let them choose.
5. **Things outside the web count too.** A film's colour grade, a shop's signage, a record sleeve, a hotel lobby: these are often better anchors than websites.

## The benchmark gallery as a study set

https://miaai-lab.github.io/Claude-Opus-5.5-100-HTML-Files/ holds 100 single-file pages, each shown with the exact prompt that produced it, and the source is at https://github.com/MiaAI-Lab/Claude-Opus-5.5-100-HTML-Files. Use it three ways:

- **Show the user** a few pages that match a direction, when words aren't landing ("something like #18, but for your bakery").
- **Study a technique** by opening the page and its source when you need to see a recipe working. `techniques.md` cites page numbers.
- **Calibrate ambition.** Before building, look at two or three pages in the same archetype and aim at that finish level.

The repository publishes no licence, so study it and link to it, but don't paste its code or prompts into client work. Write your own.

## Research for positioning (business sites, optional)

When the user names competitors, glance at two or three to see what the category looks like, so the concept can deliberately *not* look like them. Note each competitor's first-screen message and visual cliché in one line. Competitors are never aesthetic references.
