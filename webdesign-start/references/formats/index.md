# Format map

**Load at:** Phase 1 (to keep hypotheses while narrowing) and Phase 2 (to shape concepts). Load an individual format file only once it is a serious candidate.

A **format** is the kind of experience a site is: something you read, step into, flip through, play, use, or watch. The user never has to name one. Discovery (`../discovery.md`) listens for the signals below, keeps 2–4 formats as working hypotheses, and asks the questions that separate them. The format decides the architecture. The concept (`../concept.md`) decides everything that makes it singular: the idea, the material, the palette, the type and the art.

Every format renders in a **contemporary** design language by default (`../craft.md`, "Contemporary by default"). Many gallery study pages use period or illustrated styles: study their *architecture*, not their era. Period styling (for example most of the heritage-brand variants) is used only when the user asks for it.

Formats group into three **registers** (`../registers.md`): **world** (a place, story or object), **product** (an Apple-style launch or showcase) and **interface** (dashboards and apps). Discovery settles the register first, so a user leaning toward a dashboard or a sleek product page is never offered an invented world.

A site can combine formats. Usually one **primary format** shapes the first frame and overall form, and one or two **borrowed pieces** from other formats add a section: a product page with one scroll-journey section, or a heritage brand with a small maker tool.

A business site doesn't have to pick just one. It can have a primary experience and borrow one more as a section: a bakery site with a small "score your own loaf" toy, or a law firm with a clear "how a case works" explainer.

## Separating signals (the questions that split formats)

These are the axes discovery uses to narrow. Each plain-language question pushes toward some formats and away from others.

| Axis | Plain question | Pushes toward |
|---|---|---|
| **Register** (ask first when unclear) | "Should it feel like stepping into a world or story, like a sleek product launch, or like a tool people use?" | World → scene, journey, editorial, book, story, heritage, ambient, poster · Product → product film, showcase, instrument, reveal · Tool → app interface, console, bento, data reference, everyday tool |
| **Main activity** | "When someone visits, what will they mostly *do*?" | Read → editorial, story, book · Look and feel → scene, ambient, single-screen, poster · Buy → product, reveal · Browse → collection, data reference, bento · Play or make → playable, maker, generative · Understand → explainer, journey, instrument · Use regularly → everyday tool, console |
| **Time spent** | "A quick glance, a few minutes, or coming back often?" | Glance → single-screen, poster, reveal · Minutes → journey, story, scene, product · Often → tool, console, bento, reference |
| **Shape** | "Should it fit on one screen like a poster, unfold as you scroll, or turn like pages?" | One screen → single-screen, scene, instrument, ambient · Scroll → journey, editorial, product · Pages → book, story |
| **The star** | "Is there one thing that must be the star?" | A product → product, instrument, reveal · A place → scene, heritage · Work or pieces → collection, book, bento · Numbers or reach → data hero, reference, console · An idea or story → editorial, journey, story, explainer · A feeling → ambient, single-screen, poster |
| **Energy** | "Calm and quiet, or loud and bold?" | Calm → ambient, scene, heritage, editorial · Bold → poster, themed interface, playable, reveal |
| **Hands-on** | "Should visitors be able to touch, play or make something?" | Yes, make → maker, generative · Yes, play → playable, explainer · Just a little → scene, reveal, instrument · No → editorial, poster, collection |
| **Structure** | "Is there a natural path: through time, depth, steps, a process?" | Yes → journey, story, explainer · No → scene, collection, bento |
